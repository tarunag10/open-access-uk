Law changed 1 May 2026

Section 21 no-fault evictions were abolished in England on 1 May 2026 under the Renters' Rights Act 2025. Section 21 notices served before that date may still be valid — court proceedings must be issued by 31 July 2026. From 1 May 2026, all possession claims use the revised Section 8 grounds with new mandatory grounds and a raised serious-arrears threshold (3+ months). For current guidance, visit GOV.UK or Shelter.

How to challenge invalid notices

Challenge a Section 21 notice

A Section 21 notice can be challenged if the landlord has not used the prescribed form (Form 6A), has not provided the EPC or gas safety certificate, or has not protected the deposit within 30 days. Write to the landlord listing the defects.

Deposit protection rules

Your deposit must be protected in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receiving it. The landlord must also serve prescribed information. Failure to do so can invalidate a Section 21 notice and you may be awarded 1-3x the deposit amount in compensation.

Court process

If the landlord proceeds to court, you will receive a possession hearing date. You can defend the claim if the notice is invalid. Seek free legal advice from Shelter, Citizens Advice, or a housing solicitor.
